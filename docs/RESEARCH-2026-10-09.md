# Kalshi short-market research — overnight 2026-10-09

**Bottom line.** Bitcoin 15-min is a losing market for the maker bot (−3%). The +5% that earlier work showed was a
data artifact. Across all 19 active 15-minute series, resting bids on the favorite roughly break even (+0.9%,
not distinguishable from zero). Three small, believable edges survive held-out testing: **DOGE 15-min**, **BNB
15-min**, and **hourly BTC (KXBTCD)** at about +2% to +7% per dollar. Each is worth a few dollars a day at $10–$100,
not income. Stop-losses lose money; don't use them. Paper bots are now testing the three candidates.

All numbers are **return per dollar staked**, with a 95% range from a bootstrap over markets (or over hours, for
hourly BTC, because strikes in one hour share an outcome). *Train* = the first ⅔ of markets in time, *test* =
the last ⅓, which no rule was picked on. "Front" assumes we fill at the first trade at our price; "back" counts
only big sweeps (≥200 contracts) as fills, the pessimistic view.

## 1. The old BTC result was an artifact

`maker_pnl.py` kept only the newest 8,000 trades per market. Only KXBTC15M hit that cap (every market). Busy,
volatile markets, the ones where favorites lose, lost their mid-window trades, so the cached data over-represented
calm markets. On the same 600 BTC markets:

| Maker holds the favorite at | Old method (newest 8k trades) | Full tape |
|---|---|---|
| 90–95¢ | +5.2% | −2.4% |
| 80–90¢ | +11.8% | −2.3% |

Every other series in the old study was under the cap, so those results stand. The fix: `btc_study.py` fetches
every trade, and its `--fill-cut` option re-fetches any market that still hits the page cap. All 20 datasets
used here are complete.

The pattern: the deeper and more professional the market (BTC trades ~3M contracts per window, 90× DOGE), the
worse a slow maker does. Short crypto markets are close to fairly priced, which matches the published work (Le,
arXiv 2602.19520: Kalshi crypto prices under 1h are almost perfectly calibrated). With 19 markets tested, one or
two will look good by luck. BNB's range is far enough above zero, in both halves, to survive that, but its book is
thin and the pessimistic view has only 39 fills. Fills are the open question, and the paper bot is testing them.

### Hourly BTC (KXBTCD): a real favorite-longshot bias

Every price band from 80¢ to 97¢ is positive in **both** halves. Makers on the favorite: +2.7% train, +2.1% test.
Makers on the longshot side: −8% to −35%. The literature places this bias in longer markets; 15-min markets don't
show it. How a bot should trade it (`btcd_strikes.py`, no look-ahead):

| Strategy (3–57 min left, favorite 85–96¢) | Front | Back | Train / test |
|---|---|---|---|
| A bid on every in-band strike (~12 per hour) | **+2.0% [+0.5, +3.7]** | +1.6% [−0.3, +3.2] | +1.8% / +2.6% |
| First 3 strikes to fill each hour | +2.4% [+0.4, +4.2] | +1.5% | +2.3% / +2.4% |
| One order per hour (old maker.py) | +0.6% | +1.1% | −0.6% / +3.1% |

A mistake I caught: a first version picked strikes in Kalshi's listing order and showed +5.5%. Strikes are
listed ascending and the lowest always settle YES, so that rule peeked at the outcome. The table above uses time
order only.

## 4. Stop-losses and exits

Tested on the bot's own fills across all 19 series, with exits priced off the real later tape (stops sell as a
taker: −1¢ slippage plus the taker fee).

| Exit rule | Pooled | Train | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold to settlement | +0.9% | +0.5% | +1.5% |
| Stop if favorite trades ≤70¢ | −0.4% | −0.4% | −0.3% |
| Stop ≤50¢ | +0.3% | +0.3% | +0.2% |
| Stop ≤30¢ | +0.3% | −0.1% | +0.9% |
| Stop 15¢ below entry | −0.4% | −0.4% | −0.2% |
| Resting sell at 99¢ (maker, free) | +1.1% | +0.8% | +1.6% |

- **Stops cost money.** A stopped trade averages 2.6¢ worse than holding (fee + spread on the way out).
- Of trades stopped at 50¢, 45% would have won, which is about what a fair 50¢ price implies. Falling favorites
  don't keep falling, so there's nothing for a stop to catch.
- **BTC 15-min is the one exception.** There, falling favorites keep falling (they win only ~37%), and a 50¢ stop
  lifts −3.1% to −0.7%. That's still a loss, so drop BTC rather than patch it.
- The theory says the same thing (Kaminski & Lo: stops help only with momentum). Public tests found the same
  (PANews: 690 stop/take-profit combinations on BTC 15-min, none positive).
- A resting 99¢ take-profit is harmless and frees capital early. Optional.

## 5. Other angles tried on BTC 15-min (none survived held-out testing)

None of these held up: time left, bid price, time of day, longshot order size, spot distance from strike (in
std-devs), 10-min volatility, 3-min spot move, 2-min Kalshi price move, the final minute, 50/50 prices, and
early exits. Each time, the best combination on train failed on test.

Examples:
- **7.5–14 min left at 55–70¢:** +14–19% on 220 markets. It went to +1.0% on 2,871 independent per-minute
  quotes.
- **Final-minute bids:** +9% on 80 markets, gone by 200.

DOGE time-of-day hint: midnight–4 AM ET is best and noon–4 PM ET is worst in both halves. For hourly BTC the
pattern reverses (noon–4 PM best). Not acted on.

## 6. Web research, highlights (sources in the session log)

- Makers beat takers on Kalshi overall (Bürgi, Deng & Whelan: makers −9.6%, takers −31.5%; makers buying ≥50¢
  +2.6%). Favorite-longshot bias is strong in longer markets and weakening.
- Settlement rules:
  - **Crypto:** 60-second CF Benchmarks average at close vs at open; a tie goes YES; missing data resolves NO.
  - **Gold/oil/FX:** a single Pyth 1-minute print. No weekend listings.
- DOGE/XRP order books are 3–4 contracts deep at the top and sub-penny ticks allow stepping ahead. The paper
  bot only counts a fill once trades reach our price, so being stepped ahead is already modeled.
- About 100 Kalshi series now charge makers 25% of the taker fee. None of the 15-min ones do yet: check
  `fee_type` before any live run.
- No rebates for crypto markets. Basic API tier: 20 reads/s. We hit it all night running 3 pulls and 3 bots.

## 8. Recommendation

1. **Let the paper bots run 3–5 days.** Dogecoin and BNB need ~200 settled trades each, and hourly BTC needs ~100
   hours. The question is whether real fills match the simulation.
2. **Then keep only the market(s) whose paper win rate clearly beats break-even** (the report prints both).
   Most likely one of DOGE / BNB / hourly BTC.
3. **Go live at $10 for a week on that one market.** That needs your Kalshi API keys, and `bot.py --balance`
   first, because the live order path has never touched the real API.
4. **Only then scale toward $100,** at 5–10 contracts per trade with a ~$20 daily halt. Expect roughly $5–15/day
   if the edge holds, with losing days common.

Don't: trade BTC 15-min, take (cross the spread), add stop-losses, or size up before live fills confirm the edge.
